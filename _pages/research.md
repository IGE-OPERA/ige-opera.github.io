# Research

**OPERA does open computational earth and climate science, in the context of climate change.**

Our group studies key environmental and climate processes across the **atmosphere**, the **ocean** and the **cryosphere**, with methods rooted in numerical modelling, data science and open science. We develop, calibrate and evaluate process-based models; we explore data-driven and hybrid approaches; and we care about the design of the numerical systems that carry this research.

## Why we work the way we work

Computational earth and climate science is going through a period of methodological renewal. The physical models built and maintained by international communities over decades — models like NEMO, WRF-Chem, LMDZ or GEOS-Chem — remain the backbone of our understanding of the climate system and of the projections that inform society. Meanwhile, a growing family of methods — machine learning, emulation, hybrid modelling, differentiable programming — opens complementary ways of learning from the ever-richer wealth of observations and simulations.

These approaches are sometimes portrayed as rivals. Our day-to-day experience is different, and more interesting: **they need each other.** Data-driven methods gain physical credibility and the ability to extrapolate when they are grounded in process understanding; process-based models gain accuracy, calibration and new observational constraints when they embrace learning components. Many of the most promising developments of our field — hybrid climate models, learned parameterisations, neural data assimilation — live exactly at this interface.

**OPERA is deliberately built along this interface.** Part of the group contributes to community models; another part develops differentiable Earth system tools and machine-learning components, including satellite calibration/validation and simulation-based inference; and much of our work consists precisely in making them meet — coupling machine-learning components into community models, calibrating them, and learning from data. We also explore how recent AI tools can assist scientists in their daily research practice.

Rather than a choice between traditions, we see our group as a small laboratory of methodological convergence: the full spectrum of approaches is practiced here side by side, their interactions are studied as an object of research, and young scientists can learn all of them.

## Research themes

Our research themes span the components of the Earth system that our group works on:

- **Atmosphere** — atmospheric aerosols and chemistry, clouds, atmosphere–surface interactions, from process studies to regional and global modelling,
- **Ocean** — near-surface ocean processes, air–sea exchanges, ocean modelling and remote sensing,
- **Cryosphere** — sea ice, snow, glaciers and ice-sheet processes, and their coupling with the ocean and atmosphere.

All of them are addressed **in the context of climate change**: our models, our data-driven tools and our analyses aim at understanding how these components respond to, and feed back on, a changing climate.

## Advancing computational methods

Across themes, we develop new methodological material:

- **Improving process-based models** — development, calibration, evaluation and uncertainty quantification of community models.
- **Exploring data-driven approaches** — hybrid modelling, differentiable programming, neural data assimilation, simulation-based inference, satellite calibration/validation, and AI-assisted research workflows.
- **Enhancing numerical system design** — modularity and interoperability of models and monitoring systems, and a revisiting of the APIs that geoscientific models expose to their users.

## Emerging activities

Beyond our core themes, the group is opening new application domains:

- **Glaciology and paleomagnetism** — with Facundo Sapienza's arrival, OPERA welcomes research on physics-informed machine learning for glacier modelling and on statistical modelling of paleomagnetic and gravity data.

## Open science

Everything above comes with a commitment to **open source and reproducibility**: our in-house software is developed on [GitHub](https://github.com/IGE-OPERA), our experiment descriptions are reproducible by design, and we regularly assess the reproducibility of the science we publish.
